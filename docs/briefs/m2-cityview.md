## What to build
**1. Streaming.** `createCityView(world): WorldView` (interface in `src/render/worldView.ts`: `group`, `update(camX, camZ, dayFrac)`, `dispose()`). The world is cut into square chunks of 60 m (20 cells of 3 m; derive from `world.cell`, do not hard-code 3). Each chunk is at most two meshes: near (full dressing) or far (simplified), built lazily from the sim data and merged into one geometry per mesh with `MeshBuilder`. Policy: chunks whose centre is within ~170 m of the camera are near; out to ~330 m far; beyond that not present. Build at most one or two chunks per frame (a time budget of about 4 ms per frame, measured with `performance.now()`), nearest first, so streaming never causes a hitch; when the camera first appears somewhere (or teleports) build the chunk under it and its neighbours immediately. Dispose geometry of chunks that have been out of range for a few seconds; keep a small cache (LRU) so turning round does not rebuild. Chunk meshes rely on three.js frustum culling (correct bounding spheres). No visible pop within 150 m: the far version must have the same silhouette as the near one (same boxes, no windows/props), and the swap from far to near must not change the outline.
A solid or rail belongs to the chunk containing its centre/first point. Pre-bucket solids, rails and zones per chunk once at creation.
**2. Terrain.** Mesh cells with the sim's diagonal (see `src/sim/world.ts`: each cell split from its (ix, iz) corner to its (ix+1, iz+1) corner; the drawn ground must be exactly the collision ground, so never move terrain vertices). Per-surface inks with subtle per-patch variation. On top of the exact ground you may add decoration that does not change the collision shape by more than 3 cm: street centre dashes and kerb lines where STREET meets PLAZA, tram rails on TRACK (two thin ink strips), stair treads on STAIRS drawn as real steps: for each stair cell row, small risers/treads as boxes sitting on the ramp (they may stand up to 0.12 m proud of the ramp; the skater rides the ramp underneath, and the stairs are a rough surface anyway), grass tufts, dock bollard paint. WATER: the sea as flat teal with white print-dot wave bands that drift with time (pass time through vertex colours on a small animated mesh or a second cheap material; at most one extra draw call for the whole sea).
**3. Dressing solids by kind** (`Solid.kind`, `Solid.style`, `Solid.surface`, footprint and height; unknown kinds fall back to a plain box so the city task's vocabulary cannot break you). District is known from the solid's z and `world.bands`; palette per district from DESIGN section 7 (Docks teal + mango, Mercado pink + mango, Terraces mango + cobalt, Antenna cobalt + pink), each with 5–6 building inks, used so neighbouring buildings differ.
- building / house / tower: walls in a flat ink, a darker base course, a roof lip, window rows with shutters or frames (windows carry `glow`, about half lit at night, chosen by a stable hash), doors at street level, and painted-bus maximalism on the facades: big flat-colour murals/sign blocks (stripes, chevrons, sunbursts, a fish, a hand, a number) made of a few quads on some walls; roof clutter on flat roofs (water tanks, vents, satellite dishes, laundry frames) that must stay under 0.35 m tall or sit within 0.5 m of an edge so roofs stay skateable.
- warehouse: corrugated look (vertical bands), big sliding door, painted bay number; fish market variant.
- container: ISO box with door bars and a stripe; colour from style; stacks read as stacks.
- crate, kiosk, stall, shed: small props in bright inks; stalls have striped canopies.
- awning: striped canopy with a scalloped front edge (one-way solids: draw the underside too).
- wall / quay: retaining walls with a cap stone course and occasional big stencilled numbers; quay with tyre fenders and bollards.
- mast, pylon, tank, platform: lattice-ish masts (crossed beams), red/white banding in pink/white, lamps on top (glow), the needle platform with railings.
Far versions: the plain box in the wall ink with the roof colour, nothing else.
**4. Rails by kind:** handrail (pink, with posts), ledge (a painted edge strip), wire (thin, slightly sagging look is not allowed: the drawn rail must follow the sim polyline exactly), pipe (fat, with flanges), guy (thin dark cable with marker balls), fence.
**5. Zones.** For zones of kind 'stop': a bus-stop style pole with a round sign (the lead will later colour it per line; use mango) and a small shelter roof drawn as decoration only (no collision: keep it out of the street, 1 m beside the zone centre away from any STREET cell, and no taller than a sign). For 'landmark' zones nothing.
**6. Night.** Everything already tints through the shared uniforms; your job is `glow`: windows, lamps, signs, mast lights, stall bulbs strung on wires. At night the city should read as cobalt silhouettes with mango points of light.
**7. The fixture** (`src/sim/proptown.ts`, `buildProptown(seed)`): a 300 x 420 m world with a gentle hill, containing at least one of every solid kind and rail kind and surface in four district strips (set `world.bands` so each palette shows), a stair street with handrail, a terraced bit with retaining walls, a quay with sea, a few stops, and a spawn at the top looking down it. It needs no lines. It is your test bed and stays as a showcase.

## Budgets (DESIGN section 10; hard limits, measured by `npm run perf`)
Draw calls per frame ≤ 120 in total, of which the world may use at most 60; triangles per frame ≤ 180 000 in total, of which the world at most 120 000. A near chunk in a dense district should stay under ~9 000 triangles; far under ~1 200. Vertex count per chunk may exceed 65 535 (MeshBuilder switches to 32-bit indices) but should not. Chunk build time under 6 ms each on a desktop (report the worst you measure). No per-frame allocation in `update` once the chunks around the camera are built. JS added ≤ ~30 kB gzip.
